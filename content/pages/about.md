## What XMET is

XMET (Xenobiotic Reaction Metabolism) is a controlled vocabulary for describing how foreign compounds — drugs, environmental chemicals, natural products — are chemically transformed in biological systems. It is written from the perspective of a medicinal chemist reading a metabolism study: the terms are the ones used to say *what kind of reaction occurred, where on the molecule, and what it means*.

XMET is published as a [SKOS](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/) thesaurus. Every concept has a single parent, a definition, and a persistent IRI of the form `https://w3id.org/xenosite/xmet/{id}`. That IRI opens this site in a browser and returns machine-readable RDF (Turtle or JSON-LD) to software that asks for it.

## Why it helps

Metabolism data is scattered across literature, curated databases, and rule-based predictors, each with its own naming habits. The same transformation may be labelled differently in every source, and similar-sounding labels may mean different things. XMET provides a common reference point:

- **Consistent annotation.** Curators can tag observed metabolites with one agreed term instead of free text.
- **Comparable predictions.** Outputs from different metabolism predictors can be mapped to the same concepts and compared directly.
- **Readable results.** Software can present predicted or observed reactions to chemists in familiar, precise language.
- **Interoperability.** XMET concepts are mapped to related resources — RXNO, MOP, GO, ChEBI, Rhea, KEGG, MeSH — without copying their hierarchies.

## How it is organized

The hierarchy has four top-level branches:

- **[[4000213]]** — the chemical transformation itself: oxidation, reduction, conjugation, hydrolysis, rearrangement, and their refinements down to specific reactions.
- **[[4000212]]** — the conventional Phase I / II / III framing, used as tags on reactions rather than as chemical categories.
- **[[4000263]]** — independent properties of a reaction, such as mass shift, leaving group, ring or aromaticity effect, and bond cleavage, which combine with any reaction class.
- **[[4000281]]** — the small set of relationship types XMET uses between concepts.

Beyond the parent–child hierarchy, concepts can be linked across branches. For example, an oxidative dechlorination is *always with* a chloride leaving group, and a +16 Da mass shift *suggests* hydroxylation. The [relations page](schema/) lists every link type.

## What XMET is not

XMET is deliberately narrow. It is not a general chemistry ontology and does not catalogue functional groups, named reactions, or reagents unless they matter for reading metabolism. Display names that pinpoint a specific atom — "C4 aromatic hydroxylation" — are generated by software from XMET concepts plus a site position; they are not separate concepts.

## Status and stability

XMET is a working draft under active curation. Identifiers are opaque numbers that carry no meaning; they are never recycled, and retired or merged identifiers are recorded. Labels, definitions, and placement in the tree may still change as the vocabulary is reviewed. The [quality page](quality/) shows where review is most needed.

## Who maintains it

XMET is developed by the [Swamidass Lab](https://swami.wustl.edu) at Washington University in St. Louis as part of the [XenoSite](https://xenosite.org) project. Its source is openly available on [GitHub](https://github.com/swamidasslab/xenosite-xmet), where suggestions and corrections are welcome — see [Contribute](contribute/).
