# XMET Redesign Notes

This README explains the main redesign decisions in the revised XMET seed ontology and the companion validation/mapping files.

The goal of this pass was not to produce a final ontology. It was to convert the current large seed into a clearer, safer, med-chem-oriented foundation for two immediate use cases:

1. mining biomedical text for prodrugs, metabolic activation, metabolic reactions, enzymes, species, routes, matrices, and experimental systems; and
2. labeling reaction paths generated by XenoSite-Forest from reactant/product structure and rule provenance.

## Files

- `xmet-revised.yaml`: revised SKOS-first XMET seed vocabulary.
- `xmet-forest.sssom.tsv`: sidecar SSSOM mapping from XenoSite-Forest rule/ruleset identifiers to XMET terms.
- `validate_xmet.py`: lightweight validator for preferred-spine and hierarchy rules.

## Core Redesign Decisions

### 0. Existing IDs Must Not Be Repurposed

The ontology can add new IDs freely, but existing IDs should not be reused for a narrower or different meaning. If a concept needs to split or shift, mint a new ID and optionally mark the old concept as deprecated for new annotation.

This came up during the redesign:

- `xmet:2100016` was restored as `specific enzyme cue`;
- `xmet:2100017` was minted for `transporter mention cue`;
- `xmet:0001018` remains the legacy broad `amino acid or thiol conjugation`;
- `xmet:0001016` was minted for the narrower `amino acid transfer conjugation`.

Reason: changing an existing ID's meaning would break downstream mappings, assertions, old model outputs, and any curated XenoBase annotations.

### 1. Reaction Chemistry Is Separate From Enzyme Biology

Reaction classes are named by the chemical transformation, not by the enzyme family that often catalyzes it.

For example:

- use `hydroxylation`, not `CYP reaction`;
- use `glucuronidation`, not `UGT reaction`;
- use `glutathione conjugation`, not `GST reaction`.

Enzymes, genes, species, tissues, assay systems, and matrices are biological context facets. They are useful for text mining and assertion grounding, but they should not be parents in the reaction-class hierarchy.

Reason: enzyme class is orthogonal to reaction identity. A CYP can perform multiple reaction types, and a reaction type can be mediated or modeled through multiple biological or chemical contexts.

### 2. Phase I And Phase II Are Tags, Not Main Hierarchy Roots

The original seed allowed Phase I and Phase II to act like large hierarchy roots. The revised design keeps them as a small disposition-stage vocabulary:

- `Phase I metabolism`
- `Phase II metabolism`
- `Phase III transport`
- `elimination or excretion`

Reaction classes can be tagged with a disposition stage, but the phase term should not be the preferred parent.

Reason: Phase I/II language is useful and expected in xenobiotic metabolism, but it is a conventional ADME framing rather than a chemically precise hierarchy. Using it as a parent creates overly broad shelves and makes the ontology harder to reason over.

### 2b. Phase II Transfer Conjugation Is Separated From Reactive Adduct Formation

The revised conjugation hierarchy explicitly separates ordinary metabolic transfer conjugation from reactive-species capture and macromolecular adduct formation.

The Phase II-like branch is:

- `metabolic transfer conjugation`
- `sugar or glycosyl conjugation`
- `small-group transfer conjugation`
- `amino acid transfer conjugation`

The warning/adduct branch is:

- `reactive-species capture conjugation`
- `glutathione conjugation`
- `protein adduct formation`
- `DNA adduct formation`
- `cyanide trapping adduct formation`

Reason: conjugation is not one biological meaning. Glucuronidation and sulfation often support clearance, but protein/DNA adduction is toxicity-relevant, and GSH conjugation is often a warning sign for electrophile formation even when GST contributes. Detoxication, toxicity, and enzyme mediation should be asserted separately.

GSH and cyanide adduct terms include mass-spectrometry hints. For GSH adducts, the common clue is usually a **129 Da neutral loss**, not 127 Da. Parent-to-product shifts are path-dependent: many oxidative GSH adducts appear near `+305 Da` relative to the reduced parent, while direct addition to an already formed electrophile can be closer to `+307 Da`. These are evidence hints, not hard ontology inferences.

### 3. Bioactivation Was Split Into Scoped Biological Effects

The previous seed had broad `bioactivation` terms that risked implying hard relationships that are often only sometimes true.

The redesign replaces this with a `biological_effect` scheme:

- `metabolic activation language`
- `reactive-metabolite activation`
- `pharmacologic metabolic activation`
- `detoxication assertion`
- `metabolic activation effect unspecified`

These terms are assertion-level facets, not reaction-class parents.

Reason: "bioactivation" is highly contingent. It can mean formation of a chemically reactive/toxic metabolite, or formation of a pharmacologically active metabolite from a prodrug. Those are different claims and require different evidence.

Time-dependent inhibition is also represented as an assertion-level warning, not a reaction class. XMET includes terms for `time-dependent enzyme inhibition assertion`, `covalent enzyme inhibition assertion`, `mechanism-based enzyme inactivation assertion`, `suicide inhibitor assertion`, and matching text-mining cues. TDI can suggest possible reactive-intermediate or covalent enzyme-inactivation liability, but the ontology does not infer a specific adduct without supporting evidence.

The inhibition branch is intentionally separated:

- `time-dependent enzyme inhibition assertion` captures the assay/evidence pattern;
- `covalent enzyme inhibition assertion` captures the mechanistic claim of covalent modification;
- `mechanism-based enzyme inactivation assertion` captures turnover-dependent inactivation;
- `suicide inhibitor assertion` is narrower still, for classic suicide substrates/inhibitors.

### 4. Reactive Product Potential Is Soft, Not A Hard Inference

Terms such as `quinone imine product`, `epoxide product`, or `acyl glucuronide product` may suggest reactive potential, but should not automatically assert toxicity, covalent binding, or true biological activation.

The redesign uses soft tag suggestions such as:

- `possibly electrophilic reactive product`
- `possible epoxide-like reactive product`
- `possible acyl-transfer reactive conjugate`
- `electrophile capture or quenching step`
- `reactivity not asserted`

Reason: product class can guide prioritization and text mining, but actual reactivity depends on structure, site, local electronics, exposure, trapping evidence, protein adduct evidence, and experimental context.

### 5. Quinone, Quinone Imine, And Quinone Methide Were Separated

The original seed used quinone species in a broad niche way that also covered quinone imines and related species.

The revised hierarchy separates:

- `quinoid formation`
- `quinone formation`
- `quinone imine formation`
- `quinone methide formation`
- `quinone product`
- `quinone imine product`
- `quinone methide product`

These can map back to existing model behavior, but the ontology itself should not inherit the model's overly broad category.

Reason: these product families are chemically related but not identical. Keeping them distinct supports cleaner naming, better text mining, and better downstream evidence assertions.

### 6. Broad Shelves Now Have Intermediate Grouping Terms

The revised seed adds intermediate hierarchy nodes where earlier shelves were too flat.

Examples:

- product families now distinguish `carbonyl product family`, `conjugated product family`, and `glutathione-derived conjugate product family`;
- reactive-product potential now distinguishes intrinsic reactive potential, capture/quenching potential, and assertion status;
- pathway roles now distinguish path length roles from path position roles;
- text-mining cues now separate metabolite reports, metabolic-effect language, and biological-context attribution;
- biological context now separates molecular actor context, organism/sample context, and experimental-system context.
- reaction classes now add real chemistry groupings under oxidation and conjugation;
- organism/sample context now includes species, tissue/organ, matrix, and experimental-system subhierarchies.

Reason: these grouping terms make the hierarchy easier to walk and easier to validate, without adding chemical claims that are only sometimes true.

### 7. Exact Enzymes Belong In External Slims

The revised XMET ontology no longer enumerates CYPs, UGTs, SULTs, transporters, or other exact enzyme/gene/protein entities as local concepts.

XMET keeps only generic biological-context slots such as:

- `enzyme family mention cue`;
- `enzyme isoform mention cue`;
- `transporter mention cue`;
- `enzyme attribution statement`.

Exact entities should be imported for extraction from an HGNC/UniProt/NCBI Gene slim and grounded to external IDs.

Reason: XMET should not become a partial gene/protein ontology. External slims are easier to refresh, easier to ground, and less likely to drift out of date.

### 8. Preferred Spine Is Explicit

Each concept should have one preferred spine for hierarchy validation. Cross-cutting labels should use facets, mappings, or assertion properties rather than multiple preferred parents.

Current preferred spines include:

- `reaction_class`
- `product_family`
- `structural_locus`
- `biological_effect`
- `reactive_product_potential`
- `disposition_stage`
- `pathway_role`
- `text_mining`
- `biological_context`

Reason: many ontology errors came from treating cross-cutting facets as hierarchy. A preferred-spine rule catches those mistakes early.

### 9. Forest Rules Are A Sidecar Mapping, Not Ontology Terms

XenoSite-Forest rulesets, rules, and SMARTS patterns are operational machinery. They can suggest XMET labels, but they should not become public ontology hierarchy.

The revised design keeps XMET terms in `xmet-revised.yaml` and places rule-to-term correspondences in `xmet-forest.sssom.tsv`.

Recommended SSSOM predicates:

- `skos:exactMatch` only when the Forest rule and XMET term are intentionally identical;
- `skos:closeMatch` for strong but not exact rule-to-term mappings;
- `skos:broadMatch` or `skos:narrowMatch` when one side is more general;
- `skos:relatedMatch` for ruleset-level or operational associations.

Reason: model rules evolve faster than ontology terms. Keeping Forest mapping in SSSOM preserves traceability without freezing implementation details into the ontology.

### 10. Provenance, Evidence, And Entity Grounding Are Mostly External

The revised seed avoids inventing local XMET terms for provenance, evidence, source versions, and most entity identity.

Recommended external reuse:

- PROV-O, DCAT, and Dublin Core Terms for provenance and dataset metadata;
- ECO and OBI for evidence and assay evidence types;
- PubChem, ChEBI, InChIKey, DrugBank, and CompTox for chemicals;
- HGNC, UniProt, and NCBI Gene for enzymes/genes/proteins;
- NCBI Taxonomy for species;
- UBERON, BTO, CL, OBI, and NCIT for tissues, cells, matrices, routes, and experimental systems.

Reason: XMET should focus on xenobiotic metabolism reaction naming and tagging. Rebuilding mature external ontologies locally would create unnecessary maintenance burden and weaker interoperability.

## High-Level Changes Made

### Added Or Clarified

- A clearer `reaction_class` hierarchy organized around chemical transformations.
- A `biological_effect` scheme for activation/detoxication assertions.
- Time-dependent inhibition, covalent inhibition, mechanism-based inactivation, and suicide inhibitor terms as warning/effect assertions and text-mining cues.
- A `reactive_product_potential` scheme for soft reactive-product labels.
- A `product_family` hierarchy that separates quinoid, epoxide-like, reactive conjugate, and nitrogen-centered product families.
- A `structural_locus` scheme for site-localizable naming.
- A `biological_context` scheme for generic molecular actor, organism/sample, and experimental-system context.
- Intermediate grouping terms that remove broad flat shelves in reaction classes, product families, reactive potential, pathway roles, text-mining cues, and biological context.
- External extraction guidance for spaCy/LLM pipelines.
- A sidecar SSSOM mapping file for XenoSite-Forest correspondences.
- A validation script to enforce preferred-spine behavior and catch accidental hierarchy misuse.

### Removed Or Deferred

- Phase I/II as large hierarchy roots.
- Broad bioactivation as a reaction-class parent.
- Hard inference from product family to toxicity/reactivity.
- XenoSite-Forest rule/ruleset concepts inside the ontology hierarchy.
- Local provenance/evidence/source-version spines that are better handled by PROV-O, ECO, OBI, DCAT, and assertion records.
- Local exact enzyme/gene/protein/transporter concepts that are better handled by HGNC, UniProt, and NCBI Gene slims.

### Preserved As Mappable

- Existing XenoSite-Forest rule and ruleset identities are preserved through SSSOM mappings.
- Existing broad model categories can still map to more precise XMET concepts.
- Text-mining cues remain available for ambiguous terms such as bioactivation, active metabolite, reactive metabolite, quinone species, microsomes, routes, and assay systems.

## Validation

Run:

```bash
python3 validate_xmet.py xmet-revised.yaml
```

The validator checks:

- duplicate IDs;
- missing labels;
- missing schemes;
- ambiguous preferred spines;
- missing parents;
- cross-spine preferred-parent errors;
- accidental use of Phase I, Phase II, or metabolic activation as reaction-class parents;
- overly flat hierarchy shelves that may need intermediate terms.
- declared Forest SSSOM mapping files exist;
- Forest SSSOM mappings point to real XMET concepts rather than deprecated in-ontology Forest placeholders.

Current status after this redesign:

- `0` hard errors;
- `0` validator warnings.

The hierarchy audit covers every concept scheme. The only intentionally shallow areas are the small disposition-stage vocabulary, the assertion-level biological-effect vocabulary, and generic molecular-actor cues whose exact enzyme/gene/protein hierarchy is delegated to external slims.

## Design Principle

XMET should name what the reaction is chemically. Other facts should be attached as facets or assertions:

- what enzyme or system produced it;
- what species or tissue was studied;
- where the transformation occurred structurally;
- what evidence supports it;
- whether the product is pharmacologically active, possibly reactive, trapped, detoxified, or toxic;
- which model or rule suggested it.

That separation keeps the ontology stable while still making it useful for text mining, model output labeling, database annotation, and med-chem interpretation.
